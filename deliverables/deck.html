The framing I take into the build: the model is not the bottleneck. Data trust is. So the first thing the prototype proves is what it refuses to do.

The market already tells you where the ceiling is

What works

  • Aramex (Dubai, WhatsApp, since Oct 2018): "50 percent of shipment inquiries" handled by the bot, 8M+ customers, EN/AR/TR. Same market, same query mix.
  • DHL Freight VIVA: "answers 93 percent of inquiries without assistance", but that queue is mostly informational. Germany, Italy, Netherlands in 2026.
  • Klarna, month one: 2.3M chats, two-thirds of all chats, 700 agent equivalents, 11 min to under 2 min, 25% fewer repeat inquiries, $40M profit effect.

What fails

  • Klarna reversed in May 2025. The CEO: customers must always be able to reach a human. AI-only cut quality.
  • Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024 BCCRT 149: damages of CAD 650.88, and "it makes no difference whether the information comes from a static page or a chatbot". A promised slot is a binding statement.
  • DPD, Jan 2024: an AI layer went rogue after "an error occurred after a system update". Scope control beats prompt tuning.

MVP scope, and what I cut on purpose

In

  • One channel: web chat, English and Arabic, RTL aware
  • Status lookup, reschedule a redelivery, change an address
  • Identity check before any change: tracking number plus the last 4 digits of the phone on the record
  • Eligibility rules in server code, never in the prompt
  • Cleaning step with a generated audit log, 866 rows to 840
  • Operations view: blocked records, reasons, and every change made

Cut, and why

  • Voice: ASR error on Arabic dialects and UAE place names doubles the failure surface, proves nothing new
  • WhatsApp: right channel in production, but it is Meta template procurement, not engineering risk
  • OTP identity: no SMS gateway in a demo. I name the substitute instead of faking it
  • Database: in-memory shows the same guardrails
  • Refunds, claims, COD collection: money movement needs a human
  • Proactive notifications: higher value, but needs a consent and quiet-hours regime
  • Fine-tuning: nothing here is a model-capability problem

The rule that shapes the whole build

The model chooses what to ask. The server decides what is allowed. Every record change passes one gate: verified identity, clean record, eligible status, offered slot. Otherwise it goes to a human with a reason code.

Kill criteria, set before the pilot starts

  • Containment under 25% after two weeks of tuning: stop, and spend the money on the source data instead.
  • Repeat contact within 72 hours above 15%: the resolutions are not real.
  • Any unverified record change: stop the pilot the same day.

What I do not claim: a second-order saving from fewer wasted delivery attempts. It is probably the larger prize, and I have no attempt-cost data to size it. That is a question for the operator, not an estimate for a deck.
